About

I am a researcher and engineer working where high-performance computing, formal methods, and autonomous systems meet. My work spans robotics, autonomous perception, computer vision, point cloud processing, sensor fusion, automated and epistemic planning, formal verification, digital twins, and neuro-symbolic AI. The common thread is building autonomous systems that perceive, reason, plan, and act reliably, where reliably means something a proof can underwrite.

I enjoy working close to the metal as much as at the algorithmic level, with a strong focus on C++ and Python, parallel computing, GPU acceleration, and low-latency systems. Sophisticated reasoning methods are often dismissed as impractical for real systems; I am interested in the computational architectures that make them practical.

I study AI Engineering at ESCOM-IPN and Philosophy at the FFyL-UNAM. The two are not separate tracks. Questions about what a formal structure is, and what it means for one to represent a space or a state of knowledge, arrive in both.

01

ALETHEIA, epistemic planner

Planning over dynamic epistemic logic in S5n and KD45n frames. Presented at ICAPS 2026.
  • C++23
Detail

Classical planning assumes the agent knows which propositions hold. Epistemic planning drops that. The search space is not a set of valuations but a set of pointed Kripke models, each of which must be updated, minimised, and compared in full at every node, and goals may be irreducibly modal: not that the door is open but that an agent knows it is. Performance therefore reduces to three questions, namely how a Kripke model is represented, how modal formulas are evaluated over it, and how two models are recognised as the same epistemic situation.

Three answers dominate the design. A model is three flat arrays of 64-bit words for valuation, accessibility, and designation, so modal operators become word-parallel set operations rather than pointer traversals. Formulas are evaluated as extensions rather than pointwise, computing for each subformula the set of worlds where it holds, bottom-up and memoised on hash-consed formula identity, which turns common knowledge into a single greatest fixpoint instead of one graph search per world. Bisimulation contraction assigns world indices in an order fixed by the model's structure alone, so bisimilar states serialise identically and duplicate detection becomes a comparison of 128-bit fingerprints.

02

Control-oriented lane perception

Monocular segmentation scored on the geometry an MPC controller consumes, not on mask overlap.
  • Python
  • PyTorch
  • C++
  • TensorRT
  • ONNX Runtime
Detail

A lane segmenter is usually judged by how well its mask overlaps the annotation. A model predictive controller does not consume masks. It consumes the vehicle's lateral offset within the lane and the curvature of the road ahead, so those are the quantities measured here. Averaging overlap over the natural frame distribution is insensitive to exactly the frames a controller cares about, which are the tight curves.

Training uses a curvature-stratified subset of CurveLanes with a curvature-sensitive cost, which flattens the distribution and lifts the tails. The detection gap between straight road and tight curve was 21.6 points before this; it is now 3.9. Calibration is a pinhole model fitted by lane parallelism and then checked independently against the vanishing point, an arrangement where the validation does not reuse the assumption being validated.

The full chain was ported to C++, including preprocessing, the network through ONNX Runtime on a half-precision TensorRT engine, and all of the geometry. The port is pinned to the Python reference by golden vectors rather than trusted. It generalises to KITTI without retraining, recovering the lane in 94.7% of frames.

varietas, algebraic kinematics for ROS 2

Exact inverse kinematics by Gröbner bases, with completeness certified by the structure of the ideal.
  • C++
  • Eigen
  • GMP
  • ROS 2
Detail

Robot kinematics is a system of polynomial equations over a finite-dimensional quotient ring, and varietas solves it as one, using elimination theory rather than numerical iteration or structural pattern matching. The tangent half-angle substitution rationalises the forward kinematics map, a Gröbner basis of the resulting ideal is computed offline, and the inverse kinematics is resolved at runtime by eigendecomposition of a fixed-size action matrix.

Completeness is not asserted, it is certified. By the Finiteness Theorem the quotient algebra is finite dimensional exactly when the variety is finite, a condition decidable from the leading terms of the basis alone, and its dimension bounds the number of solutions counted with multiplicity. Where IKFast pattern-matches known kinematic structures and fails silently outside them, varietas either returns a solution set with that guarantee or names precisely which hypothesis of the Extension Theorem was violated.

Exactness is enforced where geometry enters rather than assumed. Buchberger's algorithm discards a critical pair when the S-polynomial reduces to zero, which is a structural test on an empty term list; over floating point, cancellation leaves a residue, the pair survives, and the finiteness verdict read off the leading monomials then describes a different ideal. The offline half therefore runs over ℚ with GMP-backed rationals. The same consideration governs the URDF front end, which recovers exact geometry over the rationals by projective approximation of quaternions, since a URDF writes π/2 as 1.57079632679 and reading that literal as a rational yields an exact answer about a robot nobody built. Validated against KDL on a KUKA iiwa.

KripCUDA, model checking on the GPU

Explicit-state model checking of Kripke structures, with CTL as a fixpoint computation over the boolean semiring.
  • C++20
  • CUDA
Detail

Model checking a branching-time logic is a fixpoint computation over the powerset lattice, which is complete and of finite height, so by Knaster and Tarski the modal operators reach their least and greatest fixpoints by Kleene iteration in at most |S| steps. Every CTL operator is then a sequence of existential pre-image applications interleaved with set operations, and the existential pre-image is exactly a sparse matrix-vector product over the boolean semiring against the transposed transition relation. That is the observation the whole implementation rests on, because it is a shape a GPU is built for.

The transition relation lives on the device in compressed sparse row form, state sets are bitmaps assembled by warp ballot without atomics, and model products are constructed entirely on the GPU rather than staged through the host. Fair cycles need strongly connected components, which are found by parallel colouring. Structural invariants are enforced at construction rather than trusted, so totality of the relation is checked and successor lists are sorted, which makes the CSR layout a function of the model alone and not of the order the caller supplied its transitions.

Every device computation has a sequential counterpart verified step by step against it.

ePlanSys, epistemic planning for ROS 2

EPDDL-based planning with dynamic epistemic logic reasoning, built on PlanSys2.
  • C++
  • ROS 2
Detail

The execution layer for epistemic planning on real robots. It extends PlanSys2 with S5 and KD45 world models, EPDDL domain support, and heuristic search over epistemic states, so that multi-robot coordination under partial observability is expressed in the planner's own terms rather than encoded around it.

This sits alongside ALETHEIA, which is the planner proper, and Epistemic-Robotics, which connects both to physical robots under ROS 2. The motivating case is small and concrete. Two robots must retrieve an object from a corridor that may be blocked; a classical planner routes the second robot through it because it treats the world as globally known, and the robot fails on arrival. Here the initial epistemic state represents that uncertainty as two designated worlds, and no route is assigned until the first robot senses the corridor and collapses the belief state.

06

Collaborative SLAM for a ground fleet

Multi-robot mapping under intermittent connectivity, where the fleet map is what has been delivered rather than what is known.
  • C++
  • ROS 2
  • Gazebo
  • SLAM
Detail

A fleet of unmanned ground vehicles explores an unknown planar environment, each agent running an independent pose-graph SLAM front end over its own laser observations. Because inter-agent connectivity is intermittent, the fleet-level map is not the instantaneous union of the individual maps. It is the union of what has actually been delivered, and this implementation makes that distinction explicit rather than assuming the link.

Individual occupancy estimates are fused by an operator that is idempotent and order-independent over a bounded join-semilattice, so the same observations arriving in any order give the same map, and delivery is gated by a per-agent link process, so a lost link freezes an agent's contribution without erasing it. The divergence between what an agent knows and what the fleet knows is then measured directly. That divergence is precisely the asymmetry the dynamic epistemic logic layer is meant to reason about, which is what connects this to the planning work.

A second iteration carries the same substrate, unchanged, onto the floor plan of a real building with three agents.

github.com/HanielUlises/ROS-Mobile · Planning/MultiRobot_ws

Epistemic-Robotics

The umbrella framework: epistemic SLAM and multi-agent planning under dynamic epistemic logic, on real robots.
  • C++
  • ROS 2
  • DEL
Detail

Autonomous robots in partially observable environments cannot plan over what they do not know. This framework grounds multi-agent task planning in dynamic epistemic logic, so that ignorance is represented rather than assumed away. Each agent maintains a Kripke model of the world, actions are epistemic events that refine the model by product update rather than state transitions, and navigation routes are winning regions of μ-calculus reachability formulas computed over the occupancy graph rather than geometric shortest paths.

The distinction the framework insists on is that an agent reaching a zone and an agent knowing it has reached it are different goals, and the planner treats them differently. Several of the projects listed here derive from this one. ALETHEIA is the planner, ePlanSys is the execution layer, and the collaborative SLAM work of the degree thesis is the perceptual substrate. This repository is where they meet physical robots.

GCN-GrabCut, automatic segmentation

Removes the user from the loop in GrabCut by predicting the trimap with a graph convolutional network.
  • Python
  • PyTorch Geometric
  • OpenCV
Detail

GrabCut needs a human to mark the initial trimap, which is what keeps it out of automated pipelines. Here the trimap is predicted instead by a graph convolutional network over superpixels, seeded by a training-free saliency prior computed from the image itself, so nothing about the method requires an annotated initialisation at inference time.

Mean IoU on DUTS is 0.584 against 0.109 for the trivial prediction, with 187,826 parameters.

Computational PDEs

Partial differential equations by classical schemes and by physics-informed networks, including the inverse problem.
  • Python
  • PyTorch
  • NumPy
Detail

Three complementary approaches to the same equations. Classical numerical schemes cover finite differences, finite elements, and finite volumes. Physics-informed neural networks embed the PDE residual together with the initial and boundary conditions directly in the loss, so the constraint is enforced by the objective rather than by the discretisation.

The inverse formulation is the interesting one. There the network is used to infer unknown coefficients, parameters, or source terms of the equation from observed data, which turns a solver into an identification method and makes the comparison against the classical schemes a comparison of what each approach assumes it already knows.

Possible-World Semantics

A machine-checked theory of possible worlds, situations, and modal necessity, formalised in Lean 4.
  • Lean 4
Detail

A formalisation of possible-world and situation semantics in the higher-order modal and situation-theoretic tradition of Zalta, Fine, and Barwise and Perry. Every theorem is checked by the Lean kernel, and every remaining gap is tabulated as an explicit open proof obligation rather than left implicit, which is the honest way to publish a partial formalisation.

The development is layered. An ontological grounding core fixes the primitives and their algebra, covering encoding, situations, truth, parthood, dependence, and abstract objects. A modality layer postulates the S5 calculus over frames and operators. A situation layer then builds worlds and their meet-semilattice on top, with extensionality and infima proved rather than assumed. It is the formal counterpart of the philosophical work.

SAT and SMT solver

A complete DPLL implementation with an extension to DPLL(T) in progress.
  • C++
Detail

A decision procedure for propositional satisfiability built as complete DPLL, with unit propagation, pure literal elimination, and backtracking search over the assignment tree. The aim is to determine whether a formula is a tautology, a contingency, or a contradiction, and to do so with a procedure whose completeness is a property of the search rather than of the benchmark set.

The extension in progress lifts this to DPLL(T), where the propositional search is coupled to a theory solver and the boolean skeleton is checked for consistency against the theory at each step. Work in progress, and marked as such.

16

egregium

Interactive visualisation of surfaces and manifolds, with curvature and geodesics obtained by exact symbolic differentiation.
  • C++
  • OpenGL
  • Symbolic differentiation
Detail

A surface is given by a formula and rendered in three dimensions together with its Gaussian curvature and its geodesics. The name is from Gauss's Theorema Egregium, since the curvature is an intrinsic invariant of the surface, fixed by its metric alone and independent of any embedding in ℝ³. Surfaces enter in four forms, namely explicit, parametric, implicit by raymarching rather than tessellation, and by first fundamental form with an optional embedding for display.

Every formula is parsed and symbolically differentiated rather than approximated by finite differences, so the quantities built on top of it are exact to floating-point precision. That matters here more than it usually does, because a finite-difference curvature on a coarse mesh produces plausible pictures that are quantitatively wrong, and the whole point of the visualiser is that the picture and the number agree.

MuMotion

Modal μ-calculus as a specification formalism for robotic motion planning.
  • C++20
Detail

A research implementation for studying fixed-point and modal logics as specifications for motion planning. The present phase establishes the logical core, namely finite Kripke structures, an explicit abstract syntax for the modal μ-calculus, well-formedness and fragment validation, and model checking over finite models by explicit Tarski and Knaster fixed-point iteration.

The formalism reproduced is that of Larocque and Liu, who adapt the deterministic fragment and its model-checking procedure from Karaman and Frazzoli. This phase contributes no new theory and says so. Its purpose is a mathematically faithful, independently verified core that the later phases can depend on, so there is deliberately no planner in it yet.

Modular implementations of classical machine learning algorithms covering supervised and unsupervised learning, model validation, clustering, dimensionality reduction, and the supporting mathematical utilities. Each module operates independently and exposes a concise interface.

Philosophy

In preparation: Metaphysical notes on the concept of space in algebraic geometry. The paper examines spatiality as relational and algebraic, determined by rings of functions, ideals, and categorical morphisms rather than by a prior manifold of positions, and brings that reading to bear on the substantivalism and relationism debate as it applies to the points of a scheme.

The question is not incidental to the technical work. A Kripke model and a scheme are both structures whose points are individuated by what holds at them, and asking what such a point is turns out to be the same question in both settings.

Areas of interest: mathematical logic, philosophy of geometry, philosophy of language.
